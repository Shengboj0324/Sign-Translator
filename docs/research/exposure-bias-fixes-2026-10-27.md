# Starve the history shortcut so signs start

The free-running collapse (0.02 top-5 against 0.92 for real clips) is best read as a **copy shortcut that exposure bias then compounds**, not as slow drift. At 24 fps with all 32 codes of the previous frame fed back, the history almost determines the next frame. The text pathway therefore matters only where the history cannot predict the next frame, which is the lexical onset, and that is exactly where free-running fails (0.07 with the real first quarter, 0.65 with the real first half). The published fixes for this pattern do not mainly rely on token-level noise, which we tried. They make the history uncopyable or absent. ChauffeurNet keeps only the current position for 50% of training examples. Progressive Transformers' counter-only variant beat its own autoregressive base by about +4 BLEU-4 on PHOENIX14T. acRNN trains on contiguous blocks of the model's own output. The literature also tokenizes far more coarsely: every discrete text-to-motion and sign-production system I found runs at about 5 temporal steps per second with 6 or fewer codes per step, and the one clean downsampling sweep puts autoregressive FID at 0.138 with no downsampling against 0.054 at 4x. My recommendation, in order:

1. Run three diagnostics that need no training.
2. Fine-tune the prior with whole-span history dropout to a learned null and a coarse-stage-only history input.
3. Add on-policy, onset-covering rollout blocks in the style of acRNN and DAgger.
4. Only then retokenize to 2-4x temporal downsampling with 3-5 stages per part.

Our guidance is also weak by published standards. CFG 1.5 in our form l_u + g(l_c − l_u) equals s = 0.5 in MoMask's (1+s) form, and MoMask uses s = 4. Raising it helps only if the text and null branches actually differ, which the first diagnostic tests. The main caveat is that no paper reports this exact onset failure in a text-to-motion prior. The diagnosis borrows from imitation learning, driving and continuous-regression sign production, so the evidence for it is indirect.

## Our prefix curve says the model continues signs but cannot start them

Four measurements constrain the diagnosis:

- Under teacher forcing the prior conditions on the word: retrieval top-1 is 0.67-0.91 of 64.
- One-step samples drawn from the real history are nearly as recognisable as the real clip (0.82-0.87 top-5 against 0.92).
- Free-running samples are not recognisable (0.02).
- Given the real first 25%, 50% or 75% of a clip, the continuations read 0.07, 0.65 and 0.87.

So the model does not lack knowledge of the signs. It lacks the ability to produce lexical content that is not already present in its history. The flattened count is 48 frames × 32 codes = 1,536 codes for a 2.0 s sign; the quoted ~1,700 corresponds to about 2.2 s.

Imitation learning has a precise name for this: the **copycat problem**. With strongly correlated actions and an observation history, "merely learning to recover the previous action very nearly maximizes the training objective". Wen et al. measure it with a "smoking gun" test: predict the policy's next action from its previous nine actions. The behaviour-cloned policy's actions were **far more predictable from its own history than the expert's**: Ant 0.66 against 6.91 (×10⁻²), Humanoid 0.18 against 6.93 (×10⁻¹). Their Walker2d agent's knee "repeats previous actions … failing to transition" ([Wen et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.14876)). ChauffeurNet describes the same failure in driving. A network trained on its past poses learns to "cheat by just extrapolating from the past", so in closed loop it stops for a stop sign only if its own history already shows deceleration, and therefore "will never stop for a stop sign" ([Bansal et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.03079)). Motion models show it too. A teacher-forced LSTM motion generator "freezes after around 60 frames" and never recovers ([acRNN](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1707.05363)). Progressive Transformers, the original continuous text-to-pose system, "suffers from prediction drift" because near-identical neighbouring frames let it copy the previous input cheaply ([Saunders et al. 2020](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2004.14874)). Our "rest stays rest" failure is the sign-language version of the stop sign that never gets obeyed.

The jump from 0.07 to 0.65 between the 25% and 50% prefixes is the most informative number we have. One note argued that the 25% result makes "cannot initiate" incomplete, because the model fails even with a real onset. My reading, an inference I have not measured, is that the first quarter of an isolated-sign clip is about 12 frames, roughly 0.5 s, and is mostly preparation: the hands travel from rest towards the sign's location. The handshape and location that identify the sign arrive later. On this reading the model can sustain lexical content once it is in the history but cannot introduce it. The "onset" that matters is then the first lexical hold, not frame 0. This reconciles the two readings and gives a cheap test: re-cut the prefix at the detected movement onset (wrist leaves rest) or at the first hold, instead of at a fixed fraction.

The three remedies that already failed fit this picture.

- **Random history corruption.** T2M-GPT's corruption replaces whole tokens that each span four frames and carry one code ([T2M-GPT](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2301.06052)). Replacing a random subset of our 32 summed codes leaves the dominant coarse stages, and so the copy path, intact. It also produces off-manifold sums the model can learn to detect and discount. Two results support this. Language models lose only about 10% under 30% random corruption of their prefixes ([He et al. 2019](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1905.10617)). Naive dropout on history inputs made imitation *worse* than no dropout: PO-Ant 830 against 1750, PO-Walker2d 283 against 592 ([Wen et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.14876)).
- **Lower temperature.** It concentrates mass on the mode, and for a copy-dominated predictor the mode is "repeat the last frame". That is the same regression to the mean that made Progressive Transformers produce "average hand shapes" ([Saunders et al. 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2103.06982)).
- **Coarse-only sampling.** Its failure argues against a tempting alternative mechanism, that noisy fine residual codes poison the summed history. The failure sits in the temporal state, not in fine-stage noise.

The strongest steelman of the alternative view is plain compounding error. Behaviour cloning, which is what teacher-forced next-token training is, has a tight regret bound of J(π) ≤ J(π*) + T²ε. Training on the learner's own state distribution reduces it to J(π*) + uTε ([Ross, Gordon & Bagnell 2011](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1011.0686)). Both mechanisms are probably active, and they need overlapping but different fixes. The diagnostics below separate them.

One warning applies to every experiment: **next-step metrics can be anti-correlated with free-running quality**. In Bengio et al.'s speech recognition table, the baseline had the best next-step frame error (15.0) and the worst decoding error (46.0). Scheduled sampling to ε = 0 traded next-step error 34.3 for decoding error 34.5 ([Bengio et al. 2015](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1506.03099)). Teacher-forced retrieval and one-step recognisability must not be used to select checkpoints. Free-running recogniser accuracy has to be the selection metric.

## Published tokenizers emit five steps per second; ours emits 24 × 32

Published systems carry far less per second than we do. Every discrete text-to-motion or sign-production system I found downsamples time about 4x and emits 1-6 codes per step:

Our 768 codes per second is **about 25x MoMask's 30**. Our 24 temporal steps per second is about 5x the norm. SignGPT rejects residual stacks for exactly this reason: L levels × 3 streams means 3L targets per step. That is argued, not ablated ([SignGPT](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.21709)).

Three measured ablations explain why the norm sits where it does.

**Downsampling rate.** MoTok's sweep with a T2M-GPT-style AR generator on HumanML3D gives text-to-motion FID 0.138-0.147 at downsampling rate 1, 0.062-0.077 at rate 2, 0.054 at rate 4 and 0.053-0.068 at rate 8. Rate 1 had the best reconstruction throughout ([MoTok](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19227v1)). Latent Motion Reasoning found the same on the T2M-GPT backbone. With no downsampling, reconstruction is twice as precise (MPJPE 16.6 against 30.3 mm) yet generation is worse (FID 0.242 against 0.137). The authors call the bottleneck "Knowledge Density", the information content per token ([LMR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.24100)). One detail matters for us: in that table, R-Top-1 was slightly *higher* with no downsampling (0.497) than at 4x (0.482). Downsampling alone bought realism, not text alignment. What improved both was a **coarse 1/4-rate plan stream generated before the full-rate stream**: FID 0.040, R-Top-1 0.537.

**Residual depth.** MoMask's generation FID is best at 5 residual layers (0.051) and degrades to 0.076 at 6 and 0.084 at 7, while reconstruction keeps improving. Quantization dropout during tokenizer training is essential: generation FID is 0.091 without it and 0.051 at q = 0.2 ([MoMask](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.00063)). On ASL, SeRV finds that level 0 alone carries most of the meaning: text-to-sign R@1 is 13.61 with level 0 against 14.52 with all three levels. Its hierarchical temporal-plus-residual generator also beats flattening all residual tokens into one sequence on both How2Sign and OpenASL ([SeRV](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.05742)).

**Which stage carries the errors.** VALL-E makes only codebook 1 autoregressive. Given the true codebook 1, its non-autoregressive fine levels reach WER 2.8 against 2.2 for ground truth, and robustness errors are attributed to the autoregressive part ([VALL-E](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2301.02111)). RQ-Transformer, the origin of our depth-transformer design, states that exposure bias "can also accumulate across depth D". Stochastic code sampling plus soft labels moved its ImageNet FID from 14.06 to 13.11 ([RQ-Transformer](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2203.01941)).

Audio shows that a 24 Hz temporal rate is not extreme by itself: Moshi runs at 12.5 Hz and MusicGen at 50 Hz. What differs is that those models put linguistic content in the first level or a semantic stream only 4-8 codes deep. Audio codec models never flatten many levels into time at length, and a one-step delay between levels recovers most of what fully parallel prediction loses: MusicGen FAD 0.96 with delay against 2.58 parallel ([MusicGen](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05284); [Moshi](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.00037)). AudioLM shows what happens without a content stream: acoustic tokens alone produce speech "akin to babbling" ([AudioLM](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.03143)). That is a fair description of a 0.02 free-running sign.

The DAgger bound turns this into arithmetic. The flattened horizon for our 2 s sign is 1,536 decisions. A SignRR-like tokenizer (4x downsampling, 3 levels per part, 4 parts) would give 12 × 12 = 144. At equal per-code error, the quadratic bound is about 113x smaller ([Ross et al. 2011](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1011.0686)). This is an upper bound, not a prediction.

Two cautions apply before retokenizing. First, no published sweep uses RVQ tokenizers or sign data, and hand articulation may need finer time resolution than HumanML3D body motion. Second, this project's own record shows that an earlier 7.5 steps/s tokenizer (v3) produced priors that never beat chance on validation, because its codes were near-uniform. Any downsampled tokenizer has to pass a learnability check on its code statistics, not only reconstruction.

On masked versus autoregressive decoding, the only same-tokenizer comparison in sign production favours non-autoregressive decoding: G2P-DDM scores BLEU-4 7.50 against 5.94 for its own AR variant on PHOENIX14T ([G2P-DDM](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2208.09141)). BAMM keeps AR's END-token length handling and adds one bidirectional re-mask pass, which moved FID from 0.064 to 0.055 ([BAMM](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.19435)). Cross-paper back-translation scores are not comparable. SignRR's table lists G2P-DDM at BLEU-4 10.78, while the G2P-DDM paper reports 7.50 ([SignRR](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.28568)).

## Exposure-bias remedies pay off when the model's own history arrives in contiguous blocks

The remedy family is large, but the measured gains concentrate in a few design choices.

**Scheduled sampling.** For each token, the gold token is fed with probability ε_i, otherwise the model's own token. The decay follows one of three schedules: linear ε_i = max(ε, k − ci), exponential ε_i = kⁱ, or inverse sigmoid ε_i = k/(k + exp(i/k)). On MSCOCO it raised BLEU-4 from 28.8 to 30.6. Always feeding the model's own samples collapsed to 11.2, and flipping one coin per sequence rather than per token "performed much worse" ([Bengio et al. 2015](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1506.03099)).

Huszár showed that the objective is inconsistent. For two tokens the loss is

ε·KL[P‖Q] + (1−ε)·(KL[P_x1‖Q_x1] + E_{z∼Q_x1} KL[P_x2‖Q_{x2|x1=z}]),

whose optimum moves toward the factorised Q = P_x1·P_x2 as ε → 0. In other words, a model trained this way learns to **ignore its prefix** ([Huszár 2015](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1511.05101)). For us that property is useful in moderation, because ignoring the copyable prefix is what we want. It argues for scheduled sampling as a regulariser at rates of 0.5 or less, not as the main objective.

**Transformer versions.** Two-pass, shared-weight scheduled sampling works only with soft or sampled feedback. Argmax feedback dropped DE-EN BLEU from 29.62 to 20.57, while Gumbel-softmax held 29.58 and improved JA-EN from 19.46 to 20.87 ([Mihaylova & Martins 2019](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1906.07651)). Parallel scheduled sampling cut CIFAR-10 Image Transformer FID from 52.39 to 41.47 at p = 0.5 after a 50% teacher-forced warm-up, and p = 1.0 failed (98.38). Its own theorem says it equals true sampling only when p = 1 and the number of passes K ≥ T ([Duckworth et al. 2019](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1906.04331)). One discrepancy is unresolved: that paper's abstract claims +1.6 BLEU on dialog, but the table read shows at most about +0.6 mean.

**Motion-specific results.** acRNN alternates 5 ground-truth frames with 5 self-generated frames and scores the self-generated steps against ground truth. It generated more than 18,000 frames without freezing, where the vanilla LSTM froze near frame 60 and scheduled sampling "also freezes eventually" ([acRNN](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1707.05363)). T2M-GPT's corruption at τ = 0.5 lifted top-1 from 0.417 to 0.491 with a smaller FID change, so its main effect was on **text alignment** ([T2M-GPT](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2301.06052)). MoScale's perturbation of coarser-scale tokens improved FID from 0.096 at γmax 0.2 to 0.046 at 0.6 ([MoScale](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.03799v2)). MotionStreamer's two-forward variant, with replacement fraction γ_t = ½(1 − cos(πt/T)), moved FID from 12.944 to 11.790 ([MotionStreamer](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.15451v3)). In sign production, Gaussian input noise and future-frame prediction raised Progressive Transformers' dev BLEU-4 from 7.04 to 11.93 ([Saunders et al. 2020](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2004.14874)).

**Video and world models.** These pair corruption with a *known level*. GameNGen samples Gaussian context noise up to 0.7 and feeds the level to the model through 10 learned buckets; without it, rollouts degrade after 10-20 frames ([GameNGen](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2408.14837)). Diffusion Forcing gives every token an independent noise level, trains with Σ_t‖ε_t − ε_θ(z_{t−1}, x_t^{k_t}, k_t)‖², and stabilises rollouts by conditioning on a slightly noisy past ([Diffusion Forcing](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2407.01392)). Self Forcing goes furthest. It trains on the model's own rollout with a whole-sequence distribution-matching loss, so the first frames are graded on what the model produces from nothing. It reached VBench 84.31 against CausVid's 81.20 at the same speed; frame-wise, teacher forcing scored 80.34 against 84.26 for Self Forcing with DMD ([Self Forcing](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.08009)).

**Sequence-level adversarial and RL objectives.** These cost more and gained less where they were measured. Professor forcing improved character-level PTB only from 1.50 to 1.48 bits per character at about 3x training time ([Lamb et al. 2016](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1610.09038)). MIXER optimises BLEU directly with REINFORCE ([Ranzato et al. 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.06732)). Its mechanics are recalled, not re-read.

| Remedy | Measured effect | Domain | Transfer to our prior |
|---|---|---|---|
| Contiguous self-feed blocks (acRNN) | no freeze over 18k+ frames; LSTM froze at ~60 | motion RNN | **high**: same freezing pathology |
| History corruption (T2M-GPT τ=0.5) | top-1 0.417 → 0.491 | text-to-motion, 5 Hz | low as tried; random subsets of 32 codes leave the copy path intact |
| Input noise / counter only (Progressive Transformers) | dev BLEU-4 7.04 → 11.93 / 11.04 | text-to-pose, full rate | **high**: same domain and frame-rate regime |
| Parallel scheduled sampling p=0.5 | CIFAR FID 52.39 → 41.47 | image tokens | medium: needs K>1 passes to approach free-running |
| Known-level corruption (GameNGen, Diffusion Forcing) | stable long rollouts | video | medium: needs a corruption-level embedding |
| Self-rollout + sequence loss (Self Forcing) | VBench 80.34 → 84.26 | video diffusion | medium-high but costly; discrete tokens need REINFORCE or Gumbel |
| Professor forcing | BPC 1.50 → 1.48, 3x cost | char LM | low |

Four lessons carry over to us. Feed the model's **own** history in **contiguous blocks** that cover the onset. Use **sampled or soft** feedback, never argmax. Keep the self-feed rate at **0.5 or less** after a long teacher-forced warm-up. **Tell the model how corrupted** its history is.

## Text has to be unavoidable at the lexical onset

Guidance is the obvious onset lever, but it has a precondition. Our sampler computes `logits = free + guidance * (logits - free)` with the free branch built from empty text and **the same history** (`signtranslator/generation/motion_rvq.py`). This is per-token CFG, l = l_u + g(l_c − l_u). MoMask and BAMM write it as (1+s)·l_c − s·l_u and use s = 4-6, which is g = 5-7 in our form; our 1.5 corresponds to s = 0.5 ([MoMask](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.00063); [BAMM](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.19435)). The precondition is that the two branches disagree. If the history already determines the next frame, l_c ≈ l_u, and any g amplifies almost nothing. The analogous language-model method shows that guidance is powerful when the branches do disagree. Context-aware decoding, p ∝ p(y|c,x)^{1+α}/p(y|x)^α (formula recalled), gave +14.3% factuality on summarisation ([Shi et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14739)). CFG let LLaMA-7B lead LAMBADA, with gains "comparable to doubling the parameter count" ([Sanchez et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.17806)).

Where to apply guidance matters too. In diffusion, guidance is "clearly harmful" at high noise and largely unnecessary at the end; restricting it to a middle interval cut ImageNet-512 FID from 1.81 to 1.40 ([Kynkäänniemi et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07724)). Sequence position is not noise level, so the direction does not transfer. The useful lesson is that a single global scale is rarely optimal. For isolated signs, recognisability matters more than diversity, so a strong scale over the predicted lexical-onset window, decaying to 1.5, is a reasonable but untested schedule. The same goes for applying higher scales to coarse stages than to fine ones, the closest precedent being MoMask's separate base and residual scales.

Calling this "anti-history" guidance is ill-posed, because our unconditional branch already contains the history. The useful third branch is a **text-only expert** that sees text, clock and duration but a null history. It can be combined product-of-experts style, l = l(t,h) + β·[l(t,∅) − l(∅,∅)]. This is my proposal, not a published method. It needs training with whole-history dropout, or the null-history branch is meaningless.

That training is the most direct remedy in the literature. ChauffeurNet kept "only the current position" for 50% of examples, forcing the network "to look at other cues in the environment" ([Bansal et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.03079)). Progressive Transformers' "Just Counter" variant removed pose inputs entirely and kept only a progress counter. It beat the autoregressive base by +4 BLEU-4 on the dev set (11.04 against 7.04), so for that full-rate sign model, its own history was a net harm ([Saunders et al. 2020](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2004.14874)). Our prior already receives a clock and the target duration, which are the natural counter to keep when the history is nulled.

Three recalled lines of work point the same way. Bowman et al.'s word dropout forces a VAE decoder to use its latent ([arXiv 1511.06349](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.06349)). VLAE restricts the receptive field of an autoregressive decoder ([arXiv 1611.02731](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.02731)). Keyframe-focused imitation upweights the loss at changepoints that the history cannot predict ([arXiv 2106.06452](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.06452)). Wen et al.'s heavier fix trains an encoder against an adversary that tries to recover the previous action:

V(E,F,D) = E[L(F(e_t), a_t)] + λ·KL(p_E(e_t)‖N(0,I)) − α·L(D(μ_{e_t}, a_t), a_{t−1}).

The adversary is conditioned on the target action, so it removes only history information that the target does not share. It beat behaviour cloning on 5 of 6 tasks, for example PO-Walker2d 1296 against 592, and approached DAgger without environment interaction ([Wen et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.14876)). The same paper's warning applies here: **span dropout to a learned null is not unit-wise dropout**, and unit-wise dropout made things worse.

Strengthening the text pathway itself has weaker support. DiT found adaLN-Zero better than cross-attention for class conditioning ([DiT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09748)), but that comparison is recalled, is for diffusion, and has no exact numbers. Modulating every temporal block with pooled text through AdaLN is cheap to add, but our evidence says the problem lies downstream of the text encoder: swapping MiniLM for flan-T5 changed nothing.

Retrieval sidesteps initiation entirely. ReMoDiffuse adds retrieved motions as extra memory and reaches HumanML3D top-1 0.510, against 0.511 for real motion. Poorly designed retrieval variants did *worse* than none on KIT (FID 0.307-0.314 against 0.245) ([ReMoDiffuse](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.01116)). In sign production, retrieve-then-refine with a shallow RVQ now leads PHOENIX14T back-translation. SignRR scores BLEU-4 10.96, and its refiner adds about +2.7 BLEU-4 over raw retrieval (8.31) ([SignRR](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.28568)). Users preferred stitched dictionary signs over Progressive Transformers 87.5% of the time ([Walsh et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.07663)). The closest published benchmark to our metric is SignAvatars' word-level R-precision, generated top-5 0.815 against 0.865 for real ([SignAvatars](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.20436)). It shows that an autoregressive VQ prior at w = 4 can get close to real on isolated signs.

## Ranked recommendations for the residual-VQ prior

Run three diagnostics before any training run. They cost minutes on the existing checkpoint, and they decide whether recommendations 1-2 or 3-4 come first.

- **Text-sensitivity profile.** Along teacher-forced and free-running trajectories, log D_t = KL(p(x_t | text, h_<t) ‖ p(x_t | ∅, h_<t)) per frame and per RVQ stage. If D_t is near zero through the preparation and onset frames, the copycat diagnosis holds and global CFG cannot help. If D_t is large but free-running still fails, compounding error dominates.
- **History-predictability test.** Port Wen's test directly. Fit a text-free predictor of frame t's stage-1 codes from frames t−9..t−1, then compare its accuracy on real clips and on free-running samples. Samples that are much more predictable than real signing confirm the copy shortcut.
- **Onset-aligned prefix curve.** Re-cut prefixes at the detected wrist-departure or first-hold frame rather than at 25/50/75%, and log per-frame wrist speed of free-running samples. This separates "never starts" from "starts the wrong sign".

**Rank 1 ranks first** because it attacks the shortcut that the measurements point to and costs one fine-tune. It is also the only fix whose closest evidence comes from a full-frame-rate sign-production model. Progressive Transformers' counter-only variant is our model with the history removed, and it won by a wide margin. Two cheap additions fit the same run: upweight the loss on frames poorly predicted from history (the keyframe-focused idea, recalled), and add AdaLN modulation of temporal blocks by pooled text plus clock (weak evidence). Success means the free-running score rises materially above 0.02 and the onset-aligned 25% continuation approaches the 50% level. If D_t stays near zero after training, add the Wen adversary.

**Rank 2** belongs in the same experiment as a second arm, because it changes only what the temporal transformer reads. The coarse-only sampling failure does not count against it. That experiment changed what was *sampled*, while this one changes what is *copyable*.

**Rank 3** targets the residual failure that rank 1 cannot fix: once the model starts a sign from its own imperfect frames, does it hold together? The block structure is the important detail. Isolated replaced frames leave runs of ground truth that the copy path still exploits. Contiguous blocks, as in acRNN, and multi-pass rollouts, per Duckworth's K ≥ T condition, close that gap. Tie the ground-truth target to the time index, so a delayed rollout is taught to jump to the sign rather than to keep drifting.

**Rank 4 is the best-evidenced change for overall generation quality, but it ranks fourth** for three reasons. It is expensive. The one study that separated alignment from realism found that downsampling alone did not raise R-precision. And this project already lost a round to an unlearnable low-rate tokenizer. If ranks 1-3 leave free-running far below the 50%-prefix level, retokenize. Pair it with a low-rate stage-1 "plan" generated before the detail stages, the variant that improved both FID and alignment in LMR.

**Ranks 5-7.** Rank 5 is nearly free, but on its own it is likely to fail for the reason D_t will show. Rank 6 is the strongest route to recognisable isolated signs, and its reference row should be in every evaluation from now on. Rank 7 is for when cheaper fixes plateau. Its structural options conflict with the owner's preference to avoid architectural change without evidence, so it needs evidence from ranks 1-4 first.

## Conclusion

The question shifts from "how do we stop drift" to "why does the history make the text unnecessary". The answer points to cheaper fixes than tokenizer redesign. Our failed experiments perturbed the history while leaving it copyable. The successful precedents removed it, replaced it with the model's own contiguous output, or told the model how far to trust it. A 2 s isolated sign is only 48 temporal steps, so full on-policy rollouts are affordable here in a way they are not for video or long-form speech. The project can test the most direct fixes within days, without touching the tokenizer.

The deeper implication concerns measurement. The teacher-forced and one-step numbers that looked healthy are exactly the kind that can rise while free-running gets worse. The prefix curve, re-cut at the lexical onset, is a better instrument for this problem than any aggregate score. It should run on every checkpoint, alongside the per-frame text-sensitivity profile and a real-clip retrieval baseline. If history dropout lifts the onset-aligned curve but free-running stays far below it, that pattern is the evidence the owner's preference asks for before restructuring the tokenizer into a coarse plan plus detail. If it fails to lift the curve, the copycat diagnosis is wrong and compounding error is the target.
