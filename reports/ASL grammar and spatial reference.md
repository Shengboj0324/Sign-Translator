# Make ASL spatial grammar explicit, measurable state

ASL grammar and spatial reference should be modelled as an **explicit discourse state that conditions the motion generator**: a referent-to-locus table, verb agreement frames, role-shift episodes, non-manual marker (NMM) spans with syntactic scope, and prosodic boundaries. Automatic geometric checks then score that state directly on the generated 3D motion. Nobody has shown that it emerges from text conditioning alone. This matters because **no published neural sign-language production system models loci, agreement or role shift**. The best text-to-3D systems on How2Sign reach only about **13–15 back-translation BLEU-4** and **5–6/10 text-alignment ratings from Deaf raters** ([SOKE](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17799v3); [Neural Sign Actors](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.02702)). The rule-based avatar literature, by contrast, repeatedly finds that spatially inflected verbs, spatially associated entities and grammatical facial expressions are what native signers comprehend better ([Lu & Huenerfauth 2012](https://aclanthology.org/W12-2909.pdf); [Kacorri & Huenerfauth 2016](https://terpconnect.umd.edu/~hernisa/pub/2016-acl-hkacorri-huenerfauth.pdf)). Every target phenomenon reduces to a quantity you can compute from How2Sign-style data (rotations, metric torso and head translation, gaze unit vectors, blendshapes): pointing-ray clusters for loci, the angle between hand displacement and the inter-locus vector for agreement, the angle between gaze and a locus (with a measured **160 ms gaze lead** and **74% vs 11%** object-gaze rates for agreeing vs plain verbs) ([Thompson, Emmorey & Kluender 2006](https://crl.ucsd.edu/newsletter/Publications/Thompson_NLLT_06.pdf)), change points in head and torso yaw for role shift, blendshape spans aligned to constituent edges for NMMs, and doubled final-sign duration plus a blink at phrase boundaries ([Tang et al. 2010](https://ling.cuhk.edu.hk/people/gladys/doc/Crosslinguistic%20variation.pdf)). The binding constraint is data. How2Sign has no released gloss or NMM labels. The grammar-annotated ASL corpora, the BU ASLLRP/NCSLGR collection and the CUNY motion-capture corpus, are research-only or request-only. So grammar labels must be transferred onto How2Sign and kept as internal training assets, and only How2Sign-derived annotations can be released.

## Eight phenomena collapse into loci, vectors, angles and spans

Each phenomenon is set out below at the level of detail a generator needs: what the structure is, which measured quantities exist, and where the literature leaves numbers missing. One pattern recurs. The *qualitative* grammar is well described. The *metric* parameters (locus angles in degrees, torso rotation during role shift, NMM lead times in milliseconds) are mostly unpublished for ASL, so they must be measured on the project's own data.

### Loci are few, contrastive and probably continuous

Signers set up a spatial reference point (SRP, or locus) for a newly mentioned referent by pointing (IX) to a location, and they refer back to it by pointing there again. When an argument is not signed overtly, **eye gaze or head tilt toward the locus may be the only identifying cue** ([Lu & Huenerfauth, CUNY corpus preprint](https://huenerfauth.ist.rit.edu/pubs/lu-huenerfauth-2013-csl-preprint.pdf)). In contrastive discourse, loci typically sit on the ipsilateral and contralateral sides of signing space, and unmarked central space counts as neutral ([Schlenker, *Semantics & Pragmatics*](https://semprag.org/index.php/sp/article/download/sp.15.1/pdf/10787)). Height is iconic: tall or powerful referents can take high loci ([Schlenker, Lamberton & Santoro](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11049-015-9323-7)). The original data support only a **three-way high/neutral/low distinction** ([Zucchi 2018](https://sites.unimi.it/zucchi/NuoviFile/Zucchi18b.pdf)).

Whether loci are discrete is contested. One camp places them on a semicircular arc at chest height. Liddell's camp lets them vary in height and distance. Either way "there are an infinite number of locations where entities may be associated" ([Lu & Huenerfauth 2012](https://aclanthology.org/W12-2909.pdf)). Lillo-Martin and Meier treat directionality as grammatical person marking, but they concede that the language must interface closely with gesture ([Lillo-Martin & Meier 2011](https://lillomartin.linguistics.uconn.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/1112/2016/11/Lillo-Martin_Meier_2011b_f.pdf)). Corpus evidence from BSL points to a **"predominantly motivated rather than arbitrary use of space"**: placement often reflects real or imagined topography ([Fenlon et al. record](https://researchportal.hw.ac.uk/en/publications/modification-of-indicating-verbs-in-british-sign-language-a-corpu/)). Generators have nonetheless worked with small discrete inventories. One BSL generator used about 6 fixed locations. Lu and Huenerfauth used **7 arc positions** marked by wall targets at fixed angles ([Lu & Huenerfauth 2012](https://aclanthology.org/W12-2909.pdf)). DePaul's Paula avatar uses **4 radial loci, at about 15° and 30° on the strong side and 30° and 45° on the weak side**; the weak side gets the extra angle to offset torso twist when reaching across the body ([McDonald et al. 2014](https://www.sign-lang.uni-hamburg.de/lrec/pub/14019.pdf)). Indexing is not marginal: it makes up about **10–15% of signing content**, and current recognition models recover it poorly ([Ranum, Hadfield & Bowden 2026, preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08056)).

The gaps are concrete. No peer-reviewed source gives the typical number of simultaneously active loci in ASL discourse, or their azimuth, elevation or distance in degrees or metres. The defensible design is therefore a **continuous 3D locus with a soft prior** (lateral modes plus neutral, three height modes), with the angular values estimated from data rather than taken from the literature. Discreteness becomes an empirical question that the project can test.

### Agreement verbs move between loci, and direction generalises better than endpoints

Padden's typology separates three classes. Plain verbs do not modify spatially. Agreeing (indicating) verbs mark person and number through their start and end locations. Spatial verbs trace a locative source and goal ([SLL abstract on Padden's classes](https://www.jbe-platform.com/content/journals/10.1075/sll.00042.kwo)). Meir adds two separate phonological tools: **path direction marks thematic source to goal, and hand facing marks the syntactic object**. That is why backwards verbs such as BORROW and TAKE move from object to subject while their syntax stays unchanged ([Meir 1998](https://signlab.haifa.ac.il/wp-content/uploads/2013/01/Meir_SLL_1998.pdf)). Arity varies by verb. ASK, GIVE and MEET agree with subject and object, while TELL and SCOLD agree with the object only ([Lu & Huenerfauth 2012](https://aclanthology.org/W12-2909.pdf)).

The most useful computational fact comes from motion capture. Across signers, absolute hand start and end points differ a lot, but **movement vectors (end − start) differ "much" less**. Lu and Huenerfauth's "vector" model fits the displacement as a third-order polynomial of the subject and object arc positions, in units where **shoulder width = 1.0**. It picks the start point by maximum likelihood under a per-verb 3D Gaussian mixture of hand location (5–6 components). It had lower cross-signer error than the endpoint ("point") model ([Lu & Huenerfauth 2012](https://aclanthology.org/W12-2909.pdf)). In evaluations with **12 native signers**, verbs synthesized this way were judged similar in quality to a human animator's ([SLTAT 2011](https://vhg.cmp.uea.ac.uk/demo/SLTAT2011Dundee/10.html)). Modification is still not obligatory. Across **1,436 BSL indicating-verb tokens**, modification was optional and conditioned by factors including constructed action ([Fenlon, Schembri & Cormier 2018](https://researchportal.hw.ac.uk/en/publications/modification-of-indicating-verbs-in-british-sign-language-a-corpu/)). Agreement should therefore be a soft constraint whose strength varies with discourse factors, not a hard rule applied everywhere. No ASL source found quantifies path length, peak speed or duration of agreeing verbs, or the proportion of tokens that are modified.

### Role shift is a head-first cascade with no published angles

Role shift (RS) is signalled by four cues: gaze breaking from the addressee, head turn, body lean or torso shift, and the character's facial expression. It has been analysed as context shift akin to quotation ([Koulidobrova & Davidson](https://www.uni-goettingen.de/de/document/download/373344eaab36d3c3d336367b011ad472-en.pdf/KoulidobrovaDavidson-SuB-attitudes.pdf)). *Attitude* role shift (reported speech and thought) differs from *action* role shift, or constructed action (CA), which does not fit a quotational analysis ([semprag sp.10.9](https://semprag.org/index.php/sp/article/download/sp.10.9/pdf/7407)). CA is graded. FinSL motion capture supports **a continuum of "strong vs weak" CA rather than discrete classes: "the more you move, the more action you construct"** ([Jantunen et al. 2021](https://jyx.jyu.fi/jyx/Record/jyx_123456789_75961)). Articulators enter in a fixed order: when narration shifts into overt CA, **the head and eyes lead and the chest and dominant hand start last** ([SignCafe2 poster](https://pure.unamur.be/ws/files/108286985/SignCafe2_poster.pdf)). DePaul's synthesis follows the same order. Joints move in sequence (eyes, neck, pelvis, spine, sternoclavicular), and **the eyes turn to the addressee's locus up to 0.5 s before the body rotates**. A contrastive shift toward the protagonist's locus is enough; the avatar need not face it exactly. In DGS only the gaze change is obligatory ([McDonald et al. 2014](https://www.sign-lang.uni-hamburg.de/lrec/pub/14019.pdf)). Classifier constructions take role shift more readily than other verb types ([UChicago dissertation](https://knowledge.uchicago.edu/record/3634)).

**No published torso-rotation angle, head-yaw amplitude or episode duration for ASL role shift was found.** The commonly described direction rule (body toward the character's locus, gaze toward the character's interlocutor) also lacks a measured primary source. Both should be estimated from data. For measurement, MediaPipe head yaw was validated against optical motion capture on FinSL and outperformed OpenFace and 6DRepNet. Pitch and roll were less reliable ([Sargano et al. 2024](https://jyx.jyu.fi/jyx/Record/jyx_123456789_100521/Description)).

### Classifiers combine categorical handshapes with analogue paths

There are three classifier types. In entity classifiers the hand stands for the whole object. Handling classifiers show how the object is held. Size-and-shape specifiers trace its extent. Handshape is iconic but categorical, while location, movement shape and speed vary continuously ([HSE classifier overview](https://ling.hse.ru/data/2019/12/08/1521178288/Classifiers%20in%20SLs.pdf)). In the studied ASL MOVE/PUT data, handling handshapes cluster on **flat-O, S and C**, and flat-O may act as a generic handling classifier ([Open Linguistics 2025](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/opli-2025-0047/html)). Huenerfauth's generator represented a scene as "placeholders". Each placeholder had a 3D centre of mass, an orientation and a set of allowed handshapes, mapped onto a volume in front of the torso. Planning operators coordinated handshape, location, gaze, head tilt and brow raise ([Huenerfauth et al. 2007](https://aclanthology.org/W07-1907.pdf)). Classifier constructions often form their own prosodic phrases ([Tang et al. 2010](https://ling.cuhk.edu.hk/people/gladys/doc/Crosslinguistic%20variation.pdf)). No source quantifies the metric scale of depiction space, so the scene-to-signing-space mapping must be learned or specified.

### Gaze marks agreement only when the hands do

Head-mounted eye-tracking of **10 native signers** gives the field's anchor numbers ([Thompson, Emmorey & Kluender 2006](https://crl.ucsd.edu/newsletter/Publications/Thompson_NLLT_06.pdf)). With agreeing verbs, gaze went to the object locus **73.8%** of the time, against **11.1%** for plain verbs. With plain verbs, gaze went mostly to the addressee (40.7%) or elsewhere (44.9%). Gaze went to the indirect object in 98.4% of ditransitives. Backwards verbs drew gaze to the **syntactic object 82.5%** of the time and to the semantic goal 0%. Spatial verbs drew gaze to the locative: **72.2% for transitive and 68.2% for intransitive**. Locative gaze was significantly **lower** in space than object gaze, and this followed verb type, not referent height. Gaze **begins about 160 ms before verb onset** and leaves the locative before the hand arrives, so it does not track the hand. These data refute the claim that gaze checks agreement for every verb. Gaze also does not mark person ([Thompson et al. 2013](https://vl2.gallaudet.edu/pdf/thompson-emmorey-luender-langdon-2013.pdf)). DGS uses it less systematically ([Hosemann, SLL 14:1](https://www.jbe-platform.com/content/journals/10.1075/sll.14.1.05hos)). One anomaly needs checking against the original: the extracted text reports "13 signers" for BORROW, which conflicts with N = 10. Default gaze is the addressee, and gaze also regulates turn-taking. No quantitative data were found for gaze during role shift or turn-taking.

### NMMs are timed to constituent edges and spread differently

Dachkovsky, Healy and Sandler coded 6 ASL and 5 ISL signers frame by frame with FACS ([Dachkovsky, Healy & Sandler 2013](https://signlab.haifa.ac.il/images/wendy%20articles/Visual_Intonation_in_two_sign_languages.pdf)). In ASL, yes/no questions carry **brow raise (AU1+2) 100%** of the time, with the head forward in 70%. Wh-questions carry **brow furrow (AU4) 100%**, with the head moving progressively forward toward the end of the phrase and **cheek raise (AU6) in 79%**. Topics take raised brows in 95–98% (the paper is internally inconsistent) and a raised head in more than 80%. Conditionals take raised brows 100% of the time, with a raised head held across the clause in 85%. NMMs "tend to change dramatically at intonational phrase boundaries", and the manual timing cues (hold, reduplication, pause) delimit the constituent that the face aligns with.

Scope follows syntax. NMMs associate with functional heads and **optionally spread over their c-command domain** ([Neidle et al. 1996](https://ling.upenn.edu/courses/Fall_1997/ling001/nmbk96.pdf)). When manual NOT is absent, the negative headshake must cover the whole VP ([Pfau talk](https://www.uni-goettingen.de/de/document/download/e56672c6baed01bfcce8384892845ebf.pdf/Roland%20Pfau-LING3.pdf)). Headshake and wh-furrow spread; brow raise reportedly does not ([Wilbur chapter](https://www.jbe-platform.com/content/books/9789027271747-bct.53.08wil)). Timing carries meaning. Brow raise *right before* an expression (about **0.2–0.3 s**) favours a focus function, while brow raise *on* an expression lasted about **0.8–1 s** in the examples ([Schlenker & Lamberton](https://lingbuzz.net/lingbuzz/005080/v5.pdf)). Brow gestures have an anticipatory onset phase before a core aligned with the manual signs ([Liu et al. FG 2013](https://webpages.charlotte.edu/szhang16/paper/FG13-ASL.pdf)). Grammatical brow distinctions survive emotional overlay through timing and spread ([Weast 2008](https://uta-ir.tdl.org/uta-ir/handle/10106/986)). Mouth morphemes add adverbial meaning, such as puffed cheeks for "a lot" and 'th' for carelessness. Their peer-reviewed inventory is thin, and the meanings of MM and CS rest on teaching sources ([Bickford & Fraychineaud](https://www.dentonisd.org/cms/lib/TX21000245/Centricity/Domain/622/Mouth%20Morphemes%20Article.pdf)). Cross-linguistic caution applies. In Auslan, headshakes occur in "just over half" of manually negated clauses and are not linguistically constrained ([Johnston 2018](https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/a-corpus-based-study-of-the-role-of-headshaking-in-negation-in-au/)). No ASL source gave NMM lead or lag times in milliseconds, brow amplitudes, or headshake frequency.

### Prosody doubles final signs, puts blinks on right edges, and absorbs sentence-final points

Phrase-internal ASL signs last about **250–350 ms** (550 ms for complex movement). Sentence-final productions are about **twice** as long. Final holds mark boundaries but are "less reliable than sign duration" ([Tang et al. 2010](https://ling.cuhk.edu.hk/people/gladys/doc/Crosslinguistic%20variation.pdf)). Boundary blinks are short and overlap the sign by less than 75%; lexical blinks overlap it by 75% or more. In Tang et al.'s ASL sample, **all 48 intonational-phrase right edges carried a blink**. Tang et al. operationalise an I-phrase as requiring at least two of four cues: doubled duration, a change in head position, a change in brow position, and a blink. This is a ready-made boundary rule. ASL separates topics from the comment with a timing break less reliably than ISL does (76% vs 98%) ([Dachkovsky et al. 2013](https://signlab.haifa.ac.il/images/wendy%20articles/Visual_Intonation_in_two_sign_languages.pdf)).

Sentence-final pointing is the least quantified phenomenon in the brief. CUNY-style annotation records each pointing reference back to an SRP (e.g., `IX-1-s:1`) ([Lu & Huenerfauth preprint](https://huenerfauth.ist.rit.edu/pubs/lu-huenerfauth-2013-csl-preprint.pdf)). But **no retrieved source quantifies how often clause-final pronoun copies occur, or when**. The defensible rule is structural, not statistical: a final IX must reuse the subject's existing locus and fall inside the final I-phrase, before the boundary blink. Its frequency should be measured on data before it is generated by default.

## Only rule-based avatars ever encoded loci; neural generators still do not

### Published systems encode loci as slots, angles or placeholders

The systems that explicitly model spatial grammar are all rule-based or notation-based. They encode loci in three ways. **Discrete slots**: Lu and Huenerfauth's 7 arc positions and the eSIGN editor, which rounded each user click to the nearest location HamNoSys can express ([eSIGN editor](https://www.sign-lang.uni-hamburg.de/esign/annualreport2003/editor2003/srcgol.html)). **Angles relative to the midsagittal plane**: DePaul Paula computes gaze yaw from the dominant shoulder and ignores height, because referents are indicated by direction ([McDonald et al. 2014](https://www.sign-lang.uni-hamburg.de/lrec/pub/14019.pdf)). **Continuous 3D placeholders**: Huenerfauth's classifier scene model ([Huenerfauth et al. 2007](https://aclanthology.org/W07-1907.pdf)), and Dicta-Sign's SiGML extension, which replaced HamNoSys's discrete values with numeric locations ([Elliott et al., SLTAT 2011](https://vhg.cmp.uea.ac.uk/demo/SLTAT2011Dundee/8.pdf)). LIMSI anchored loci to the pelvis so that role shift could rotate the whole referential frame ([McDonald et al. 2014](https://www.sign-lang.uni-hamburg.de/lrec/pub/14019.pdf)). Agreement verbs are uniformly a citation form plus a locus-conditioned displacement. Role shift is a procedural rotation layered on the spine, neck and eyes, keyed to loci, with gaze leading. For NMMs, Kacorri and Huenerfauth built the most rigorous data-driven pipeline. It covers five syntactic types as MPEG-4 brow and head parameters. Each type is split by context (whether a brow raise precedes it; single-word vs phrase scope) and synthesized only within the NMM's syntactic span. A Continuous Profile Model latent trace averaged across recordings was **rated significantly higher than a single real exemplar** by 17 Deaf and hard-of-hearing participants ([Kacorri & Huenerfauth 2016](https://terpconnect.umd.edu/~hernisa/pub/2016-acl-hkacorri-huenerfauth.pdf)).

The data-driven generators are Progressive Transformers, G2P-DDM, T2S-GPT, Neural Sign Actors, SOKE, SignAvatars and SignLLM. They condition on text or gloss and emit keypoints, SMPL-X parameters or VQ tokens. Facial expression is just more output dimensions. **None models loci, agreement, role shift or NMM grammar** ([Neural Sign Actors](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.02702); [SOKE](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17799v3); [SignAvatars](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20436); [T2S-GPT](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07119v1)). Neural Sign Actors runs diffusion over SMPL-X with an anatomical graph neural network and a CLIP text embedding. On How2Sign it reports body MPJPE 35.87 mm and BLEU-4 13.12, but ASL-fluent raters gave it **5.8/10 for text alignment, against 8.7 for ground truth**. SOKE uses per-part VQ tokenizers plus retrieved dictionary-sign tokens to reach BLEU-4 14.48. One partial exception exists. Zhang et al. (CHI 2025) use GPT-4o to predict sentence-type NMM labels (yes/no, wh, conditional, negation) with **precision 0.91 and recall 0.97**, then blend eyebrow curves into retrieved clips. The system still has no locus model ([Zhang et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.05661v1)). Gaze is missing from SMPL-X pipelines altogether, even though gaze is the one obligatory role-shift cue in DGS and leads body rotation.

The Deaf-user evidence explains why this gap matters. Native signers rated animations with spatially inflected verbs and spatially associated entities as easier to understand ([Lu & Huenerfauth 2012](https://aclanthology.org/W12-2909.pdf)). Huenerfauth's classifier generator beat a Signed English baseline on grammaticality, understandability, naturalness and a scene-matching task with 15 native signers. Their most common request was more facial expression and more gaze movement ([Huenerfauth et al. 2007](https://aclanthology.org/W07-1907.pdf)). A deployed DGS avatar study in 2025 found that fewer than 50% of sentence units were understood, and participants named static faces and missing mouthings as causes ([Wasserroth et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.05358v1)).

### No validated metric yet scores spatial or grammatical correctness

Standard evaluation of sign-language production combines back-translation (BLEU, ROUGE, chrF, BLEURT, likelihood), DTW joint errors, FID or FVD, and small Deaf-rater studies. A 2025 meta-evaluation with 13 native deaf raters found segment-level Spearman correlations of **≈0.76 for a tuned DTW variant, against ≈0.21 for BLEU-4**. Human inter-annotator κ was only **0.36**, and the authors called back-translation likelihood the most consistent metric ([Jiang et al. 2025, preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.07453v1)). No metric in it addresses space. The only validated NMM-specific automatic metric is Kacorri and Huenerfauth's **phase-segmented DTW** over brow and head parameters. It splits the stream into four phases at milestones tied to sign timing. Its correlations were **ρ = −0.38 with comprehension and −0.33 with noticing the expression**: moderate, but significant ([Kacorri & Huenerfauth 2015](https://aclanthology.org/W15-5106.pdf)). For space there are only proxies:

- Lu and Huenerfauth's endpoint and vector error against held-out signers, in shoulder-width units, with inter-signer variation as the floor ([Lu & Huenerfauth 2012](https://aclanthology.org/W12-2909.pdf)).
- Huenerfauth's matching task ([Huenerfauth et al. 2007](https://aclanthology.org/W07-1907.pdf)).
- DePaul's proposal to use the inventory of role-shift cues as an evaluation checklist ([McDonald et al. 2014](https://www.sign-lang.uni-hamburg.de/lrec/pub/14019.pdf)).
- BackTranslation2.0's "grammatical correctness" dimension on BSL, whose internals were not available ([BackTranslation2.0, preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28673)).

The human-evaluation protocol is well established. It screens for native or early signers, gives instructions in ASL, and uses 1–10 scales for grammar, understandability and naturalness. It adds comprehension questions plus a "did you notice" question for specific NMMs, and compares against lower baselines (neutral face, uninflected verbs) and upper baselines (human animator, motion capture) ([Kacorri & Huenerfauth 2016](https://terpconnect.umd.edu/~hernisa/pub/2016-acl-hkacorri-huenerfauth.pdf); [Huenerfauth & Kacorri 2014 stimuli](https://www.sign-lang.uni-hamburg.de/lrec/pub/14010.html)).

## Six formulations turn grammar into angles, spans and change points

The formulations below are mostly **derivations**, built from the linguistic findings above. The exceptions are the vector model, the yaw-derivative headshake detector and the multi-cue boundary rule, which come from the literature. No published algorithm was found for unsupervised locus clustering on 3D ASL pointing, for role-shift detection with reported accuracy, or for NMM recognition from ARKit/MediaPipe blendshapes. Every threshold marked "tuning" is a starting value, not a literature value.

**Body frame.** All quantities live in a chest-centred body frame B. The origin is at the sternum or mid-shoulder point, +x points toward the dominant side (mirrored for left-handers), +y is up and +z is forward. Lengths are normalised by shoulder width s_w, matching Lu and Huenerfauth's units. With torso translation T(t) and rotation R_T(t), a world point maps to p_B = R_T(t)ᵀ(p − T(t) − o_sternum). Azimuth is φ = atan2(x, z) and elevation is θ = atan2(y, √(x² + z²)). The metric torso and head translation already in the How2Sign pipeline makes this frame directly computable.

**1. Locus estimation and clustering.** Pointing frames are those with an extended index finger, the other fingers flexed, low wrist speed for at least 3 frames, and the wrist forward of the torso. Each frame gives a ray r_i(s) = p_i + s·d_i, where d_i is the unit vector from the MCP joint to the fingertip. Several pointings at one referent give a least-squares locus:

```
x* = (Σ_i A_i)⁻¹ Σ_i A_i p_i,     A_i = I − d_i d_iᵀ
```

Alternatively, keep only direction, with μ = normalise(Σ d_i). Within a discourse, cluster pointing directions with a **Dirichlet-process von Mises–Fisher mixture**, because the number of loci K is unknown and differs per discourse. Online, a new point joins locus k with probability ∝ n_k·vMF(d | μ_k, κ), or opens a new locus with probability ∝ α (a Chinese-restaurant process). DBSCAN with a 10–15° angular eps (tuning) is a cheaper baseline. For generation, sample a new locus from a prior learned over pooled centroids and push it away from existing loci: φ_new = argmax_φ min_k ∠(φ, φ_k). Pooling centroids across discourses, after per-signer normalisation, also tests the theory. A BIC-selected small K with sharp modes supports the discrete arc. A diffuse distribution that correlates with real-world referent geometry supports Liddell's and Cormier's motivated-space view.

**2. Verb–locus consistency.** Inside an agreeing-verb span [t₀, t₁], with dominant-hand reference point h(t), the source and goal loci depend on the verb class. A regular verb runs from subject to object, a backwards verb from object to subject, and a spatial verb from locative source to goal. The facing target is always the syntactic object ([Meir 1998](https://signlab.haifa.ac.il/wp-content/uploads/2013/01/Meir_SLL_1998.pdf)). The derived metrics are:

```
Δh = h(t₁) − h(t₀),  u = (L_goal − L_src)/‖L_goal − L_src‖
c_dir = ⟨Δh/‖Δh‖, u⟩,             e_dir = arccos(c_dir)
e_end = ∠(h(t₁) − o, L_goal − o)  (directions, not distances: hands rarely reach the locus)
f     = ⟨n_palm(t₁), unit(L_obj − h(t₁))⟩
L_agr = λ₁(1 − c_dir) + λ₂·e_end² + λ₃(1 − f),   λ₁ > λ₂
```

Weighting direction above endpoints follows the vector-model finding ([Lu & Huenerfauth 2012](https://aclanthology.org/W12-2909.pdf)). The start-point term applies only to double-agreement verbs; for TELL, the start is lexically fixed at the chin. The sign of ⟨Δh, facing⟩ gives a free classifier: path and facing are roughly parallel for regular verbs and roughly antiparallel for backwards verbs.

**3. Gaze–locus alignment.** Let e(t) be the eye centre (head translation plus a fixed offset) and g(t) the gaze unit vector. Then:

```
α_k(t) = arccos⟨g(t), unit(L_k − e(t))⟩       for each locus k and for the addressee A
k*(t)  = argmin over {loci, A} of α, if below τ (≈8–15°, tuning); else "other"
G_verb = 1  if α_obj < τ for ≥ 50% of frames in [t_on − 160 ms, t_on + ½·dur]
L_gaze = Σ_{t∈W} w(t)·α_target(t)²,   W = [t_on − 5 frames, t_off]
```

The 160 ms window and the per-verb-type targets come from Thompson et al.: object for agreeing verbs, syntactic object for backwards verbs, a lowered locative for spatial verbs, and no penalty for plain verbs. Thompson et al. measured gaze-height offsets in monitor grid cells, which do not convert to degrees. The locative elevation offset should therefore be estimated as median(θ_gaze | locative) − median(θ_gaze | object) on the project's data. The aggregate rates (≈0.74, 0.83, 0.72, 0.68, 0.11) serve as both generation probabilities and corpus-level sanity targets ([Thompson et al. 2006](https://crl.ucsd.edu/newsletter/Publications/Thompson_NLLT_06.pdf)).

**4. Role-shift detection.** The per-frame feature vector is y(t) = [ψ_T, ψ_H, x_torso, α_A, affect-PCA₁…₃]: torso yaw, head yaw, lateral torso translation in metres, the angle from gaze to the addressee, and the first principal components of departures from the affect blendshapes. All features are z-scored against a per-signer narrator baseline. Apply Bayesian online change-point detection or PELT with a Gaussian cost. Then label segments with a hidden semi-Markov model over {Narrator, RS_k}, where each RS_k mean is tied to the azimuth sign of locus k. Because the head and eyes lead, RS onset is the earliest change in α_A or ψ_H, and the torso is expected to follow. CA intensity is the continuous weighted norm I_CA(t) = ‖[Δψ_H, Δψ_T, Δx_torso, dψ_H/dt]‖_W, thresholded into strong and weak only if needed, following Jantunen et al.'s continuum. Head yaw is the most trustworthy input, and pitch and roll should be down-weighted ([Sargano et al. 2024](https://jyx.jyu.fi/jyx/Record/jyx_123456789_100521/Description)).

**5. NMM detection from blendshapes.** The mapping from FACS action units to ARKit/MediaPipe names below is derived for this report; **no published ASL-to-blendshape mapping exists**. Detection runs in four steps. First, compute robust z-scores against a per-signer neutral baseline. Second, low-pass filter at about 3–4 Hz (tuning). Third, apply hysteresis thresholds with a minimum duration of about 150–200 ms, which keeps the 0.2 s "right-before" brow raise detectable. Fourth, snap edges to the nearest sign boundary, because NMMs align with constituent edges. The model to aim for is a multi-track linear-chain CRF or HSMM over {onset, core, offset} × marker, with one chain per articulator family (brow, head, mouth) so that overlapping markers such as topic plus wh can co-occur. The headshake detector follows Neidle et al.: count sign alternations of the smoothed yaw derivative with symmetric excursions. That approach achieved 95% negation detection on small test sets ([Neidle et al. 2009](https://www.bu.edu/asllrp/papers/Neidle_ESSLLI_2009.pdf)).

| Marker | FACS correlates (literature) | Blendshape and head features (derived) |
|---|---|---|
| Brow raise: y/n, topic, conditional | AU1+2, often AU5 | mean(browInnerUp, browOuterUpL/R) + eyeWide*; head pitch up (topic/cond) or forward (y/n) |
| Brow furrow: wh | AU4 (+AU6 in ASL) | mean(browDownL/R) + cheekSquint*; subtract k·BR to remove AU1+4 "worried" brows; head +z ramping to phrase end |
| ASL squint | AU6 + AU10 | cheekSquint*, eyeSquint*, mouthUpperUp* |
| Negation headshake | head rotation | yaw oscillation: ≥2 sign changes of dψ_H/dt above an amplitude threshold |
| Mouth morphemes | MM, PUFF, TH, OO | mouthClose + mouthPress*; cheekPuff; tongueOut (check MediaPipe's label file); mouthPucker/Funnel |

The FACS correlates come from [Dachkovsky et al. 2013](https://signlab.haifa.ac.il/images/wendy%20articles/Visual_Intonation_in_two_sign_languages.pdf) and [Bickford & Fraychineaud](https://www.dentonisd.org/cms/lib/TX21000245/Centricity/Domain/622/Mouth%20Morphemes%20Article.pdf). Blendshape names come from [Apple ARKit](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arfaceanchor/blendshapelocation) and [MediaPipe Face Landmarker](https://ai.google.dev/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/face_landmarker).

**6. Scope and boundary as structured losses.** A marker m whose syntactic span covers signs i…j gets a target activation a_m(t) = 1 on [onset(sign_i) − δ_lead, offset(sign_j)] and 0 elsewhere. The loss is L_m = Σ_t (â_m(t) − a_m(t))², plus a boundary-sharpness term at I-phrase edges. δ_lead is 160 ms for gaze. For brows it must be estimated, because no ASL lead time was found. The boundary indicator inverts Tang et al.'s rule, B(t_i) = 1 if at least two of {dur_i ≥ 2·dur_internal, a blink in the right half of the sign or the following transition, |Δbrow| > θ_b, |Δhead| > θ_h} hold ([Tang et al. 2010](https://ling.cuhk.edu.hk/people/gladys/doc/Crosslinguistic%20variation.pdf)). Generation applies the rule in reverse: at each planned boundary, time-warp the final sign to about ×2, insert a blink with less than 75% overlap, and reset or switch NMMs.

The BU ASLLRP collection is the only openly searchable ASL resource with **frame-level onsets and offsets for both anatomical and grammatical NMMs**. The anatomical fields cover head position and movement, brows, eye gaze, eye aperture, mouth, cheeks, shoulders and body lean. The grammatical fields cover negation, wh, yes/no, rhetorical question, topic/focus, conditional, relative clause, role shift (`rs:John`), and subject and object agreement. Values carry start and end points plus graded intensity, such as `+raised` or `lwrd`. Loci are subscripted in glosses (`i:GIVE:j`, `neu:GIVE:j`, `POSS-3p:i`), and gaze toward a locus is indexed the same way, with definite (point) gaze kept apart from indefinite (region) gaze ([Report 11, Neidle 2002](http://www.bu.edu/asllrp/asllrpr11.pdf)). That makes it a direct supervision source for NMM span detectors and an indirect source for verb–locus pairs. It assumes 30 fps, the same rate as How2Sign. Two traps need care. First, the Hugging Face NCSLGR mirror is tagged "mit" and loads from a DePaul mirror ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ncslgr/ncslgr)). That tag conflicts with BU's no-redistribution terms and should be treated as non-authoritative. Second, BU's terms forbid "any patents and copyrights based on this material" without permission. Nothing in the terms says whether trained model weights fall under that clause.

ASL-LEX supplies no grammatical labels. It is still valuable for separating a sign's lexical location (body-anchored vs neutral space) from locus-driven modification. Its licence conflict (OSF CC BY 4.0 vs CC BY-NC 4.0 on the website) should be resolved with the maintainers before any commercial use ([Sehyr et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7977685/)). How2Sign's own paper notes that its unreleased glosses would not have marked grammatical facial expressions anyway ([How2Sign paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.08143)). CUNY is the **only corpus combining measured 3D trajectories with SRP and inflected-verb annotation**, so it is the only ground truth for whether a verb endpoint matches an established locus ([Huenerfauth & Lu 2010](https://huenerfauth.ist.rit.edu/pubs/huenerfauth-lu-2010-lrec.pdf)). The licensing pattern dictates the architecture. ASLLRP- and CUNY-derived labels stay internal as training and validation assets. Releasable artefacts are limited to How2Sign-based annotations under CC BY-NC 4.0.

## Recommendations: build the grammar layer in six validated stages

**Stage 1. Plan explicit discourse state from English.** Before any motion is produced, a planner should emit a typed structure:

- a referent table with locus assignments;
- for each verb, its class (plain, agreeing regular, agreeing backwards, spatial) and arity;
- NMM spans with scope;
- role-shift episodes, each with its character and interlocutor;
- I-phrase boundaries.

Use an LLM constrained by rules. Zhang et al. showed this works for sentence-type NMMs at precision 0.91 and recall 0.97 ([Zhang et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.05661v1)). The locus policy should give new re-mentioned third-person referents contrastive ipsilateral and contralateral loci, use height only for iconic reasons, and point at present referents in their real direction. Verb class flags are not in ASL-LEX, so curate them from ASLLRP gloss indices (`i:VERB:j` patterns) ([Report 11](http://www.bu.edu/asllrp/asllrpr11.pdf)) and from Padden's typology. The repository already has `signtranslator/planning/loci.py`, `locus_assignment.py` and `generation/signing_space.py`, which are the natural homes for this state.

**Stage 2. Condition the generator on the state, not just the text.** Encode each locus as a chest-frame unit direction plus elevation class. DePaul's midsagittal-angle formulation is scale-invariant across signers ([McDonald et al. 2014](https://www.sign-lang.uni-hamburg.de/lrec/pub/14019.pdf)). Pass agreement as a (source, goal, facing) triple per verb span, and pass NMM and role-shift spans as framewise tracks. Add a dedicated head, gaze and face channel, since SOKE-style per-part tokenizers and Neural Sign Actors' graph denoiser both leave room for one ([SOKE](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17799v3); [NSA](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.02702)). Schedule role shift as a residual cascade: gaze first, up to 0.5 s ahead, then head, then torso, then hands.

**Stage 3. Train with masked geometric losses.** Apply L_agr inside agreeing-verb spans, weighting direction above endpoints ([Lu & Huenerfauth 2012](https://aclanthology.org/W12-2909.pdf)). Apply L_gaze in the window from 160 ms before verb onset to verb offset ([Thompson et al. 2006](https://crl.ucsd.edu/newsletter/Publications/Thompson_NLLT_06.pdf)). Add a scope loss per NMM family and the boundary terms. Keep agreement soft, since BSL corpus data show that modification is optional ([Fenlon et al. 2018](https://researchportal.hw.ac.uk/en/publications/modification-of-indicating-verbs-in-british-sign-language-a-corpu/)). Watch sampling diversity on NMM channels, because averaged traces beat single exemplars in Deaf ratings ([Kacorri & Huenerfauth 2016](https://terpconnect.umd.edu/~hernisa/pub/2016-acl-hkacorri-huenerfauth.pdf)).

**Stage 4. Measure the missing numbers on real How2Sign first.** Run the detectors from the previous section (pointing to DP-vMF loci, gaze to locus, role-shift change points, blendshape NMM spans, boundaries) on real How2Sign motion. This gives reference distributions for locus azimuth and elevation, torso yaw during role shift, brow lead time, final-sign lengthening and the frequency of sentence-final IX. The literature supplies none of these. The real-data distributions become both the priors for generation and the acceptance bands for generated output.

**Stage 5. Validate detectors and labels against annotated corpora.** Train and score the NMM detectors on ASLLRP spans, using span IoU and boundary F1 with a signer-independent split. The Neidle-group baselines are small (20–44 test sequences), so report confidence intervals ([Neidle et al. 2009](https://www.bu.edu/asllrp/papers/Neidle_ESSLLI_2009.pdf)). Request CUNY to validate locus geometry against measured SRP tracks ([LATLab corpus](https://latlab.ist.rit.edu/corpus/)). Then hand-annotate a small How2Sign subset with loci, verb classes, NMM spans and role-shift episodes in ELAN. This is the one grammar layer the project can legally release, under CC BY-NC 4.0 ([how2sign.github.io](https://how2sign.github.io/)). Before releasing weights trained on ASLLRP labels, email BU (Prof. Neidle) to settle whether the "no patents and copyrights based on this material" clause covers them ([DAI terms](http://www.bu.edu/asllrp/dai-terms.html)).

**Stage 6. Close the loop with Deaf raters and validated metrics.** Run the automatic suite on every checkpoint. It has five parts:

- e_dir and e_end distributions per verb class against real-data bands;
- the locus re-reference consistency rate (the same referent hits the same angle);
- gaze-target proportions per verb type compared with 0.74, 0.83, 0.72, 0.68 and 0.11;
- Kacorri-style phase-segmented DTW on brow and head channels ([Kacorri & Huenerfauth 2015](https://aclanthology.org/W15-5106.pdf));
- back-translation likelihood with an open model ([Jiang et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.07453v1)).

Then confirm with a Huenerfauth-protocol study. Native signers rate grammar, understandability and naturalness on 1–10 scales and answer comprehension and "notice" questions. The lower baselines are uninflected verbs and a neutral face; the upper baseline is real signers ([Huenerfauth et al. 2007](https://aclanthology.org/W07-1907.pdf)). Inter-rater κ is about 0.36, so validate each spatial metric at segment level against multiple raters before trusting it as a training signal ([Jiang et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.07453v1)).

## Conclusion

Grammatical correctness in signing space can be checked from geometry alone, without glosses. A generated clip either points twice at the same azimuth for the same referent or it does not. Its hand displacement either aligns with the inter-locus vector or it does not. Its gaze either reaches the object locus about 160 ms before the verb or it does not. Once the planner's discourse state is explicit, the grammar layer becomes testable on unlabelled How2Sign output. That sidesteps the field's two weakest points: unreleased glosses and back-translation metrics that barely track human judgement. The cost is a hybrid commitment, with structure decided symbolically and motion realised by learning. The evidence favours that commitment: every comprehension gain in the Deaf-user literature came from making space and the face explicit, not from smoother motion.

The literature's limits shape the agenda as much as its findings. The parameters a generator most needs are absent for ASL: locus angles and distances, role-shift rotation, NMM lead times, and the frequency of sentence-final pointing. Measuring them on calibrated How2Sign motion (Stage 4) is therefore both an engineering prerequisite and a likely publishable result: corpus-scale metric descriptions of ASL spatial grammar that the theoretical debate over discrete versus gestural loci currently lacks. The licence landscape decides what can ship. The model can learn from ASLLRP and CUNY, but only the How2Sign-derived layer, and the weights once BU confirms they are allowed, can leave the lab.
